## Why this matters

Meetings are where the owner's biggest deliverables come from (meeting notes, agendas, action items). A meeting is
also the recording the owner can least afford to lose: an hour of audio must survive a crash, a phone call, a dead battery or
iOS killing the app. Upstream MacParakeet's ADR-019 design (a session lock file plus audio that stays readable up to
the last second) is the model.

## Scope

- **In scope:** a meeting recorder and recovery service (`ChirpAudio` / `ChirpFeatures`), session folder files under
  `media/<id>/` (additive), VAD chunking and live-chunk transcription, finalization with diarization, a migration for
  meeting fields (`userNotes`, audio retention), the Meeting screen, Notes tab, speaker rename, recovery UI ("Partial
  audio" badge), Live Activity for recording, contracts `meeting-session-v1.md` and recovery.
- **Must not change:** file and dictation behavior; scheduler semantics; existing migrations; the rule that nothing is
  deleted without the user (retention deletes only per the user's setting and never a locked or unfinished session).
- **Out of scope:** Ask and summaries (M4), calendar integration, system audio (impossible on iOS), echo cancellation.

## Carried from M0/M1 review

- Diarization running inside the scheduler's interactive slot delays meeting finalize (the whole-branch M0/M1
  review flagged this as a latency risk, not a correctness bug). When finalization is designed, confirm diarization
  either runs outside the interactive slot or its cost is accounted for in the finalize-latency budget.

## Steps

### Step 1: Decide the crash-safe audio format on the device

Build a small DEBUG harness: record 3 minutes, kill the app (`xcrun devicectl device process terminate` or Xcode
stop) at 2:30, relaunch, and check what is readable. Compare (a) fragmented AAC `.m4a` with 1 s fragments (upstream)
and (b) segmented CAF/WAV chunks (platform research). Record results in `docs/research/<date>-meeting-crash-format.md`
and pick one. Write `spec/contracts/meeting-session-v1.md`: folder files, lock schema, states.

### Step 2: Session lock and recorder

Port `recording.lock` (states `recording`, `awaitingTranscription`; the engine route captured at start) and the
recorder writing into `media/<id>/`. Start only from the foreground (iOS refuses to start recording in the background);
`UIBackgroundModes: audio` keeps it running. Pause/resume and mute. Live Activity with elapsed time and Stop.

**Verify:** tests with a fake stream: lock written before the first audio buffer; state transitions; files closed on
stop.

### Step 3: Recovery

At launch, discover sessions whose lock says `recording` or `awaitingTranscription`: offer Recover (repair the audio,
run finalization with the captured route) or Discard (with confirmation). A recovered meeting shows "Partial audio".

**Verify:** recovery tests with fixture folders (synthetic audio); device: kill mid-meeting and recover.

### Step 4: Live preview (VAD chunks)

Port Silero VAD chunking via FluidAudio (Silero on CPU, as upstream, to keep the Neural Engine free): chunks 2–10 s cut
on speech end, fixed 5 s / 1 s-overlap fallback after 3 VAD errors, silence-only chunks dropped, RMS guard. Each chunk
is a `.meetingLiveChunk` job; the assembler offsets and de-duplicates words. Preview only; the final pass is
authoritative.

### Step 5: Finalization

On stop: lock → `awaitingTranscription`; `.meetingFinalize` job over the whole recording; diarization; segmenting;
only the custom-word clean-up step (upstream rule for meetings); save; lock removed. The Meeting screen goes to the
Transcript.

### Step 6: Notes tab, speaker rename, retention

Notes while recording and after (saved with the session and in a migration field). Rename speakers ("Speaker 1" →
a name) stored per transcript. Audio retention setting (keep forever by default; delete after N days never touches a
locked or unfinished session).

### Step 7: The "Room" meter decision

The canvas's "Room" meter has no source. Measure on the device whether a second reading (voice-isolation input vs
the raw mic level) is meaningful; if not, drop the meter. Record the decision in the design handoff and spec/04.

## STOP conditions

- Any design that could delete or overwrite a recording without the user.
- Background recording stops on the device in a way no documented API fixes.
- The owner's iOS version blocks background finalization and there is no owner decision on waiting for the
  foreground vs CPU fallback.
- A needed capability or entitlement (for example App Groups for the widget) requires an account change.

## Maintenance notes

- The lock file is written before any audio and removed only after the transcript is saved.
- VAD stays on CPU to avoid Neural Engine contention with the live chunks and the final pass.
